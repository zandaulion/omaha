Pocket Omaha helps self-directed, long-term investors understand what changed in the companies they follow and revisit the reasons behind each investment idea.

Start with a focused watchlist. See a measured fundamental score across solvency, profitability, valuation, growth and capital returns, then open the underlying evidence when something needs a closer look.

REVIEW WHAT NEEDS ATTENTION

The Review screen brings forward companies with newly recorded financial changes or reviews that are due. Record whether your reasons still hold, need watching or have changed, and keep a dated history of your own assessments.

WRITE DOWN YOUR REASONS

Save why you follow a company, what must remain true and what would make you reconsider. Add personal notes, conviction and an optional target entry price. Reconsideration conditions are checked by you; the app does not pretend to evaluate your personal thesis automatically.

TRACE EVERY SCORE

The company scorecard uses reported financial data and a traffic-light checklist of fundamental measures. Tap supported labels to read what a metric means, how it is calculated and where it has limitations. Missing data is shown as unreported instead of being replaced with a favourable estimate.

RESEARCH THE DETAILS

Explore financial trends, balance-sheet strength, margins, cash flow, valuation measures and a DCF sandbox where you control the assumptions. Compare up to five companies side by side, or filter the companies already in your research set.

KEEP WATCHLISTS ORGANISED

Create multiple watchlists, add or remove companies, sort the current list and view its aggregate fundamental picture. Pocket Omaha does not use position sizes and is not a brokerage portfolio tracker.

YOUR RESEARCH STAYS PORTABLE

Personal research is stored on the device and can be exported as a JSON backup. No brokerage connection is required. Optional AI analysis is clearly separated and controlled from Settings.

Pocket Omaha is a research and review tool. Scores summarise historical reported fundamentals; they do not predict returns or provide personalised investment advice. Market and filing data can be delayed, incomplete or unavailable.

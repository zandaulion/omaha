# 14 — Data Sourcing and the Play Release

> **Status**: research complete, no code written. Supersedes nothing; extends
> doc 13 §8 with verified numbers and a concrete provider contract.
> **Verified**: 2026-08-22, against the live SEC APIs and current Play policy.

This doc exists because two questions turned out to have the same answer.
"May an individual developer publish this to Google Play?" and "what do we do
about the market data?" both resolve to: *the release is not gated on
permission, it is gated on where the statements come from.*

---

## 1. Play Store eligibility

**There is no credential gate on investment apps.** This was the open worry,
and it is settled.

Google's Financial features declaration sorts apps into five categories —
banking and loans, payments and transfers, purchase agreements, trading and
funds, and support services. Of these, **only personal-loan apps must upload
licensing documentation.** Nothing in the Financial Services policy requires
broker-dealer registration, investment-adviser status, or an organization
account for an app that analyses securities. The one flat prohibition that
touches this space is binary options trading, which is not a feature here and
should not become one.

This is worth stating explicitly because the health policies work differently
and the contrast is misleading. Google's health declaration routes anything
resembling disease management into a *Medical apps* category that expects the
developer to be a healthcare provider or to hold regulatory clearance — a bar
an individual cannot clear. **Finance has no equivalent.** Reasoning by analogy
from a health-app rejection to this app gives the wrong answer.

### What the release does require

| Requirement | State | Where |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Financial features declaration completed | not started | Play Console |
| Visible in-app AI-generated content labelling | **done 2026-08-24** | `web/app.js` |
| Explicit "not investment advice" framing | **done 2026-08-24** | `web/index.html`, `web/app.js` |
| Privacy policy covering data handling | check before submit | Play Console |

> **Both in-app items closed 2026-08-24**, as `docs/16_ROADMAP.md` phase 1.
>
> The framing lives in four places, weighted by where a person is most likely to
> mistake output for advice. Settings carries the canonical statement — it leads
> with what the app *is*, as this section asks: the same arithmetic over filed
> statements for every user, and thesis fields that are the user's own writing.
> The DCF panel carries a short note, because a "fair value" that moves with a
> slider is the single most advice-shaped number in the app. The AI analysis
> carries one. Onboarding carries one line, so the expectation is set before the
> first score is ever seen.
>
> The AI audit found **exactly two** rendering surfaces — the deep-dive dashboard
> and the overview teaser — and both now carry an `AI-generated` tag. The
> dashboard's tag sits at the top rather than in the footer, since a label
> reached only after reading is a label reached after believing. The single
> `Generated by Gemini` caption this section describes was the teaser's; the
> dashboard had nothing above its meta bar.
>
> What remains is the Play Console declaration, which is a form, not code.

**Declare generously.** The app has no trade execution, but the watchlist and
portfolio aggregator are portfolio management, and the Gemini analysis is
adjacent to financial advice. Both belong in the declaration. Declaring a
capability costs nothing here — no license attaches to either — while
under-declaring is a documented and repeated cause of rejection. The failure
mode to avoid is an inaccurate declaration, not an expansive one.

**The advice disclaimer is a real gap.** Doc 13 §7 already calls for explicit
"not investment advice" framing and it has not landed. The app presents 0–100
grades, DCF fair values and buy targets; it needs the framing both for policy
and on its own merits. Note that the thesis fields — conviction, target buy
price, sell triggers — are *user-entered*, and the scores are algorithmic and
identical for every user. Neither constitutes personalised advice, and the
disclaimer should say what the app is rather than merely disclaim.

**AI labelling** currently amounts to one `Generated by Gemini` caption on the
summary card. Play requires disclosure in the interface rather than buried in a
policy document. Audit every AI-derived surface, not just that card.

## 2. Statements move to SEC EDGAR

Doc 13 §8 chose to ship Yahoo and defer this, with a migration trigger of
"sustained 429s or a schema break." **That trigger should be brought forward to
precede the public release**, for reasons that are only partly about resilience.

Yahoo's endpoints are unofficial and its terms do not permit automated access.
A self-hosted invite-only PWA and a public Play listing are materially different
postures, and the difference does not turn on whether the app is free. SEC EDGAR
is the officially provided alternative, offered for exactly this use, and it
carries the filed number rather than a re-derivation of it.

### Cost and access rules — verified

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Price** | $0. No API key, no registration, no quota tier, no commercial-use fee |
| **Rate limit** | 10 requests/second, per IP. Exceeding it returns 429 and can earn a temporary block |
| **Identification** | A `User-Agent` naming the project with a contact email is **mandatory** — requests without one are refused |
| **Etiquette** | Cache locally, ~100 ms between calls, do not re-fetch what you hold |

Android fetches on-device, so the 10/sec budget is per user rather than shared
across the install base. This is a structural advantage of the existing
architecture and an argument against ever consolidating Android behind a
server proxy for convenience.

### Coverage — better than doc 13 assumed

`company_tickers.json` (796 KB, fetch once and cache) maps **10,403 tickers to
CIKs**. Every ticker exercised in testing so far is present, foreign issuers
included:

```
AAPL  320193      BMBL 1830043     NOK   924613     NU  1691493
PATH 1734722      SBSW 1786909     TAL  1499620
```

All 17 metrics the scoring engine needs — Altman's five inputs, Piotroski's
signals, ROIC components, the checklist thresholds — are present in Apple's
`us-gaap` facts. Coverage is not the obstacle.

### The obstacle: two taxonomies

Foreign private issuers file 20-F but mostly report under **IFRS**:

| Ticker | Entity | Taxonomy | Tags |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| TAL | TAL Education Group | `us-gaap` | 430 |
| NOK | Nokia Corporation | `ifrs-full` | 401 |
| SBSW | Sibanye Stillwater | `ifrs-full` | 277 |
| NU | Nu Holdings | `ifrs-full` | 151 |

So `FIELD_MAP` needs a second arm. Spot-checked against Nokia, 9 of 10 core
concepts map cleanly:

| Concept | us-gaap | ifrs-full |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Revenue | `Revenues`, `RevenueFromContractWithCustomerExcludingAssessedTax` | `Revenue` |
| Net income | `NetIncomeLoss` | `ProfitLoss` |
| Total assets | `Assets` | `Assets` |
| Total liabilities | `Liabilities` | `Liabilities` |
| Equity | `StockholdersEquity` | `Equity` |
| Current assets | `AssetsCurrent` | `CurrentAssets` |
| Current liabilities | `LiabilitiesCurrent` | `CurrentLiabilities` |
| Operating cash flow | `NetCashProvidedByUsedInOperatingActivities` | `CashFlowsFromUsedInOperatingActivities` |
| Gross profit | `GrossProfit` | `GrossProfit` |
| Capex | `PaymentsToAcquirePropertyPlantAndEquipment` | **unresolved** — IFRS filers spread this across several variants |

This is the same shape of work as the alias resolution already in `yahoo.js`.
A second dictionary, not a second architecture.

### An unexpected correctness win

EDGAR labels the **reporting currency explicitly, per fact**. Nokia's revenue
returns `units: ['EUR']`.

This is precisely the defect class that contaminated 7 of 20 watchlist tickers
during the earlier audit, where traded currency and reporting currency were
conflated because Yahoo re-derives figures without reliably distinguishing
them. Sourcing statements from EDGAR removes that whole category of bug by
construction — an argument for the migration independent of everything above.

## 3. The contract to implement

**The seam already exists.** `core/providers/index.js` separates
`statementSource` from `quoteSource` exactly so this can be one new file and
one changed line. Nothing above that boundary knows the word "Yahoo".

Write `core/providers/edgar.js` exporting:

```js
async function fetchFundamentals(ticker)
// -> { annual, quarterly, latestReported, reportingCurrency }
```

matching `yahoo.js:307`. Then in `index.js`:

```js
const statementSource = edgar;   // was: yahoo
const quoteSource = yahoo;       // unchanged
```

Implementation notes:

- **Ticker → CIK** needs the `company_tickers.json` map, cached. This is a new
  lookup step Yahoo did not require; it belongs in the provider, not above it.
- **`companyfacts` is ~3.8 MB per company** (Apple). `companyconcept` returns a
  single tag in ~2 KB. Fetching 17 concepts separately is 17 requests against a
  10/sec budget; one `companyfacts` is a single request but a large payload.
  On-device and cached, the single blob is likely right — but measure on a real
  handset before committing, since parsing 3.8 MB of JSON in QuickJS is the
  part that could disappoint.
- **Non-SEC registrants are out of scope.** A company listed only in Frankfurt
  or Tokyo files nothing with the SEC. Decide deliberately whether such tickers
  are unsupported or fall back to Yahoo; do not let it be decided by accident.

### What stays on Yahoo

Quotes, prices, search and peers. No free licensed source exists for these, and
doc 13's economics still hold: licensed provider free tiers are per-key rather
than per-user and exhaust at single-digit install counts.

But the economics are worth re-costing *after* this migration rather than
before. Once statements come from EDGAR, Yahoo's remaining surface is far
smaller, and a paid tier priced for that reduced volume is a different
proposition from one priced to carry everything.

The point of the migration is that a Yahoo outage becomes **degrading rather
than fatal**: solvency, moat quality, growth, capital allocation, the full
checklist and every chart survive. Only the live price and the valuation pillar
are lost.

## 3a. Implementation notes — built 2026-08-24

`core/providers/edgar.js`, with `providers/index.js` making the choice per
ticker. Three things measured or found here contradict what §2–3 predicted, and
each changed the implementation.

### The endpoint numbers were both wrong

§3 left the endpoint choice open and asked for it to be measured. Measured:

| | doc 14 estimate | measured 2026-08-24 |
|---|---|---|
| `companyfacts` per company | ~3.8 MB | 0.91 MB (NOK), 3.61 (AAPL), **7.53 (JPM)** |
| `companyconcept` per tag | ~2 KB | **~18 KB** |

`companyfacts` is still right, by a wider margin than the estimates implied. The
engine reads ~30 fields with several candidate tags each, and **which tag a
filer uses is not known in advance** — that is the problem this module exists to
solve. Resolving it through `companyconcept` means a request per candidate, most
of them 404, at 18 KB each against a 10/sec budget.

The open risk is parsing, not fetching: `JSON.parse` of the JPM blob costs
129 ms under Node on a laptop, and JPM is twice the size §3 planned for.

### `github.com` in the User-Agent is a 403

Same second, same client, against `www.sec.gov`:

```
403  PocketOmaha/1.0 (https://github.com/zandaulion/omaha; …)
403  PocketOmaha/1.0 (github.com/zandaulion/omaha; …)
200  PocketOmaha/1.0 (zandaulion/omaha; …)
200  PocketOmaha/1.0 (https://example.com; …)
```

Not URLs in general, and not the contact address — the literal substring.
Citing the project repository is exactly what §2's "identify honestly" advice
leads a developer to write, so the most conscientious version of the header is
the one that fails. It fails only against the live host; every fixture-backed
test passes.

`data.sec.gov`, which serves `companyfacts`, is more permissive. The ticker map
is on `www.sec.gov`, so the strict host is on the critical path.

### EDGAR throttles with 403, not 429

The same User-Agent that had just been served 200 returned 403 during a burst,
then 200 again seconds later. The shared `kindForStatus` maps 403 to
`unauthorized`, which is correct for Yahoo — an invalidated crumb, recoverable
by re-establishing the session — and wrong here, where there is no session and
`unauthorized` is not transient. Unmapped, a throttled request surfaces as a
permanent failure and no caller ever backs off. `edgar.js` maps it explicitly.

### The tag map needed a rule §2 did not anticipate

§2 predicted the work was "a second tag dictionary" and that capex is the tag
needing hunting. Both true. What it missed is that **choosing between candidate
tags cannot be done by taking the first one present.**

Nokia files `Revenue` — three facts, all from a 2018 filing, covering 2015–17 —
and `RevenueFromContractsWithCustomers`, with 24 and current, having switched at
IFRS 15. First-non-empty returns a revenue series that stops in 2017 and nulls
every modern period. The same transition exists in us-gaap between `Revenues`
and the ASC 606 tags, so this is a property of the data rather than a Nokia
quirk.

Candidates are ranked by how recent their newest fact is, then by count, then by
declared order. One tag is still chosen rather than several merged: the two
sides of a revenue-recognition change are different definitions, and a series
spanning both yields a CAGR over neither.

### Periods are defined by income statements, not balance sheets

XBRL facts are either durations (`start` and `end`) or instants (`end` only).
Balance sheets are filed every quarter regardless of the period being reported,
so letting instants create periods produced **70 annual periods for AAPL**
against the 19 it has. Durations establish the period set; instants attach to
dates already claimed.

Annual is judged on reported duration, 340–400 days, rather than on `fp: 'FY'`.
Annual figures appear inside 10-Q filings and some filers stamp `FY` on a
nine-month cumulative — the number of days between two dates is a fact about the
period, `fp` is a label about the form.

### What EDGAR does not carry

Yahoo synthesises `freeCashFlow`, `ebit`, `ebitda` and `totalDebt`; XBRL has no
such concepts because companies do not file them. They are derived, and only
where the derivation is standard and every input is present — otherwise `null`,
per the rule the README opens with. A bank is the visible case: JPM gets revenue
and net income from EDGAR and `null` for free cash flow and total debt.

### Verified end to end

Against live EDGAR through the whole pipeline, not only the parser:

| Ticker | Source | Reporting | Health | Checklist |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| AAPL | EDGAR (us-gaap) | USD | 66 | 12/12 measured |
| NOK | EDGAR (ifrs-full) | EUR | 52 | 12/12 |
| SAP | EDGAR (ifrs-full) | EUR | 72 | 12/12 |
| NESN.SW | **Yahoo fallback** | CHF | 47 | 12/12 |

NOK exercises the reporting-versus-traded split — EUR statements, USD listing —
which is the correctness win §"An unexpected correctness win" describes. NESN.SW
is not an SEC registrant, returns `not_found`, and falls through to Yahoo, which
is decision 3 below working as resolved.

### Still to measure

**Parsing on a handset.** §3's caution stands and is the one open risk: 7.5 MB
of JSON through QuickJS on a phone is unmeasured, and doc 13 §20 records that
handset and emulator figures move in opposite directions from intuition. Test it
on the device, as §3 says, not on a laptop.

The Android engine bundle grew from 85 KB to 96 KB, and the ingest bundle from
20 KB to 32 KB, which is the cost of carrying both providers.

## 4. Open decisions

1. **Does the release wait for EDGAR?** The recommendation here is yes.
2. **Identify honestly to Yahoo?** SEC requires a `User-Agent` naming the
   project and answers instantly when given one. Worth testing whether Yahoo's
   endpoints still respond without a spoofed desktop agent — if they do, the
   ingestion path becomes uniformly above-board and doc 13 §8 can be rewritten.
3. ~~**Non-US-listed tickers** — unsupported, or Yahoo fallback?~~
   **Resolved 2026-08-24 to Yahoo fallback**, by the phase 0 decision in
   `docs/16_ROADMAP.md`. Chosen for coverage rather than competition: a holding
   listed only outside the US has to keep scoring. EDGAR is primary, Yahoo sits
   behind it, and the fallback triggers on any EDGAR failure rather than only on
   a missing registrant — an outage should degrade to the older source rather
   than to no statements. See §3a.
4. **Monetisation** — free app with paid Gemini analysis is a sound model and
   raises no policy problem. It is not, however, a substitute for item 1.
